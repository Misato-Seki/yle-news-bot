Unemployment insurance contributions set to increase next year

https://yle.fi/a/74-20179459

2025-08-26

The board of Finland's Unemployment Fund has proposed raising unemployment insurance contributions for both employers and employees by 0.6 percentage points next year. Under the proposal, the employer's average unemployment insurance contribution would rise to 0.92 percent of salary, up 0.3 percentage points from 2025. According to the fund, the need for an increase is due to a difficult unemployment situation and a worsening economic outlook. In mid-August, Prime MinisterPetteri Orpo(NCP) urged the Unemployment Fund to keep increases moderate.

Finland sees surge in Ukrainian homebuyers as many plan to stay

https://yle.fi/a/74-20179425

2025-08-26

Ukrainians have become one of the leading groups of foreign buyers in the Finnish housing market. Last year, Ukrainians ranked third in overall property purchases, and sixth in housing shares. Most properties have been bought in South Karelia, South Savo, and Uusimaa. Russian housing purchases in Finland have fallen sharply in recent years, mainly because of restrictions such as tighter financial controls.

Think tank slams government income tax reductions

2025-08-26

Kalevi Sorsa Foundation finds that next year's income tax cuts will benefit the wealthiest workers the most. The government approved the reductions this past spring. According to Tax Administration figures, 211 individuals in Finland earned one million or more in 2023. The marginal rate cut will benefit some 106,000 individuals pulling down more than 100,000 euros a year, the think-tank says.

Tuesday's papers: Finnish fury over Italian trash, schooled by Spain and anxious kids

https://yle.fi/a/74-20179355

2025-08-26

Finland has been importing trash for its waste-to-energy facilities to be burned for district heat and electricity generation. In total, Finland received 450,000 tons of waste last year. Some of it passed through the port of Valkom in Loviisa, where last summer and autumn were marked by deep dissatisfaction among local residents. Two-thirds of the waste imported from Italy originates in Sicily and Campania in the south, where organised crime is problematic.
