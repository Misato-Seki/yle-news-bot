Police complete probe of Finnish berry firm suspected of trafficking Thai workers

https://yle.fi/a/74-20183251

2025-09-16

Police have completed their investigation into a human trafficking case involving the berry company Arctic Group. Authorities suspect that Thai berry pickers brought to Finland by the Sotkamo-based company were exploited as victims of human trafficking. Police believe thefirm took advantage of the pickers' vulnerable position and unsafe circumstances. The case has now moved to the prosecutor for consideration of charges. If charges are filed, it will proceed to a district court trial.

Rapidly declining population forecast paints bleak picture for Finland's future

https://yle.fi/a/74-20183208

2025-09-16

Finnish consulting firm MDI has published a new population forecast for the next quarter century. According to the forecast, Finland already appears to be a largely dying country. The bleakest development will hit around 100 municipalities, where the number of schoolchildren is expected to fall by one-third by the early 2030s. However, there may be hope in Finnish Lapland, where people are turning to larger growth centres.

One-third of girls experience online sexual violence, survey reveals

https://yle.fi/a/74-20183185

2025-09-16

Save the Children Finland surveyed 4,106 children aged 11–17 in February-March 2025. One in three children had been contacted online by an adult or someone at least five years older. More than half of the respondents had not reported the sexual abuse they encountered online. The organisation operates a Finnish hotline calledNettivihje, a tip service where anyone can report suspected material or activity related to online sexual violence against children.

Tuesday's papers: Juggling jobs, Finland's latchkey kids, and hoarders

https://yle.fi/a/74-20183141

2025-09-16

In 2014, about 90,000 Finns took on extra work in addition to their main employment. By 2024, this number had risen to 138,000. On average, people spend nearly 12 hours per week on side jobs. Denmark has the highest proportion of side-job workers in the Nordics. Finland and Norway are roughly tied, and Sweden ranks last.
