From cosy to colossal: Finland's shift to large daycare centres stirs concern

https://yle.fi/a/74-20178100

2025-08-18

Jyväskylä has been at the forefront of a trend towards ever-larger daycare centres. In recent years the city has opened six facilities for between 170 and 250 children. Across Finland, large daycares have become more common since 2010. The National Agency for Education says there were 30 centres with more than 250 children last year. In Helsinki, the number of facilities with more. than 150 children grew from 28 in 2021 to nearly 40 last. year.

Some welfare regions install fridge and shower sensors in seniors' homes to cut care costs

https://yle.fi/a/74-20178079

2025-08-18

Sensors are being installed on everyday home fixtures such as refrigerators, coffee makers, showers, microwaves, toilets, beds, and front doors. These devices help relatives and home care staff monitor daily routines, such as whether meals are being eaten, and assess how seniors are managing at home. Changes to the law will require care plans to assess whether a client's needs can be met with technological solutions.

Monday's papers: Finland's lost decades, a buyer's market and getting caught red-handed

https://yle.fi/a/74-20177944

2025-08-18

Finland's economy hasn't bounced back from the 2008 crash. Between 2007 and 2023, output per person actually fell by 7 percent. Sweden and Denmark have been moving in the opposite direction, posting gains of 18 and 19 percent. A new generation of economists said the reasons are obvious: Finland hasn't been investing enough in quality education across the board. The share of young people with higher education in Finland has now fallen below the OECD average.
